WAR ACROSS THE OLD WORLD

Ri

ILacross the Old World, mighty armies march to war behind their valiant vanguards in pursuit of conquest and
destruction. Where they pass, the very ground trembles with the thunder of their armoured feet, and in their wake

rivers run dry and farmland turns barren, consumed by the ravenous hunger of the marching hosts. As dawn light breaks,
battlelines form, ordered regiments and undisciplined warbands gather beneath fluttering pennants and grisly battle

standards, their ranks stretching as far as the eye can see.

The Vanguard Approaches

The March To Battle

For every vast battle that rages across the World of Legend, a
hundred skirmishes are fought between roving warbands. Far
ahead of every marching army, brave vanguards ride. Tasked
with scouting the unfamiliar landscape, these bold companies
seek out safe passage through hostile terrain whilst driving
away the enemy patrols sent to observe their movements.

Beyond the safety of city walls, desperate outlaws stalk the
well-trodden trade routes that link the great nations of the
Old World. They clash with mercenary caravan guards, and
battle with companies of river and road wardens dispatched
from the towering cities to hunt them down. Elsewhere, on
the borders of powerful empires, militias raid rival territories,
sowing dissent and threatening the unstable peace upheld by

rival kings and emperors.

Meanwhile, far from the trappings of civilisation, in the
foothills of looming mountains, under the eaves of the endless
forests, and beneath the darkling skies of the frozen north,
tribes of brutal Orcs, savage Beastmen and bloodthirsty
Marauders battle for supremacy. With every victory,
their numbers swell until, emboldened by success,
their warlords turn their wrath upon the nations
of Men, Dwarfs and Elves, and their mighty
hosts descend upon the Old World.

Warhammer: the Old World is a game in which large armies
march to war, and yet, whilst games played with armies of
2,000 points or more are the most popular, not every game
need be so large for not all battles in the World of Legend are
fought between such mighty hosts. Many are the battles fought
between far smaller forces, and the rules contained within this
book have been created to add greater depth to such games.

For newcomers to the Warhammer: the Old World hobby,

the opportunity to start small allows them to learn the rules
and gain valuable experience more easily, whilst experienced
hobbyists can treat collecting an army for Battle March as an
opportunity to work on an exciting new project. Smaller forces
are also the ideal place to let one’s imagination and creativity
run riot, exploring new techniques and experimenting with
complex modelling projects.

OLD WORLD CAMPAIGNS

No matter how different they first appear, all wars can pivot upon the bold actions of
small forces. Two such campaigns that rage across the Old World exemplify the need
for a general to treat every victory as essential.

The War Of Settra’s Fury

When Settra, great king of the Land of the
Dead, raised his hosts to march against

the Old World in the year of IC2276, the
campaign of vast battles he had planned to
fight quickly became a long and grinding
war of attrition in which large-scale conflicts
gave way to swift and bloody skirmishes.
Whilst the skeletal legions that formed
Settra’s mighty vanguard initially moved
quickly, crushing the armies of the Border
Princes arrayed against them with terrible
efficiency, the armies that followed in

their wake were to become entrenched in
prolonged sieges and petty skirmishes, their
advance hampered by a myriad of enemies.

Behind the vanguard, it was mighty Settra
himself that led his armies towards the
coastal town of Matorea. Despite the
spirited defence of the ancient walls put
forth by a host of knights from Bretonnia,
the very land that had fuelled the great
king’s rage, the city soon fell into ruin,

and Settra led his armies in their glorious
advance. But whilst the eternal king of
ancient Nehekhara had planned to march
north-west towards yet more victories,
through the Border Princes, into the
towering Vaults and onwards to Bretonnia
beyond, fortune conspired against him,
placing furious resistance and unfavourable
terrain in his path.

The enemies that had defied Settra at
Matorea fled northwards, an army of
Bretonnian dogs that ran to skulk behind
the walls of Louen Keep, mocking the
king with their refusal to bow before him.
Forced to divide his armies, Settra led

a host to besiege and destroy the keep,
whilst trusting his underlings to continue
his conquests of the mountains. Yet even
as Settra led his host onwards towards
the fortress of Louen Keep, the displaced
inhabitants of the Border Princes began
returning to their devastated homes.

)

A War Of Attrition

Throughout the Border Princes, vicious
warlords desperate to reclaim their
abandoned strongholds and reassert their
power rallied their troops and fell upon
the armies of Nehekhara. Many skirmishes
were fought and many easy victories
claimed, for with Settra’s attention turned
elsewhere, the slowly advancing columns
of skeletal infantry, cavalry and chariotry
seemed strangely unconcerned about
holding the towns they had conquered.

In some cases, the Undead seemed
unaware they were even under attack, so
preoccupied were their commanders with
the great king’s goals that they neglected
the plight of their rearguards.

By necessity, whilst Settra occupied himself
with the destruction of his enemies at
Louen Keep, his armies began to divide,
princes and priests taking command

of advancing columns, the better to
navigate the high passes and deep valleys
of the Vaults. Thus with diverse enemies
ahead and behind, and with the great
host of Nehekhara slowly diminishing,
the unstoppable advance of Settra the
Imperishable became a confusing and
chaotic war fought on many fronts.

As summer turned to autumn and then
winter, the advance of the Undead hosts
through the Vaults became a slow and
debilitating war. More warlords joined the
fighting, amongst them the Dwarf lords

of the mountain holds, whilst the Undead
sought and plundered ancient temples

and mausoleums, rebuilding their strength
where they could. The fighting became ever
more diffuse, with roving warbands clashing
wherever rival forces met. The terror of
Settra’s invasion had waned, giving way to

a grim era in which violence was the only
constant and no corner of the Old World’s
south-eastern frontier was left untouched.

The Coming Of The Chainmaker

In the spring of IC2272, when Frydaal
the Chainmaker returned to her Norscan
tribe from her wanderings in the Chaos
Wastes, bedecked in the twisted finery of
a champion of the Ruinous Powers and
surrounded by a palpable aura of power
and menace, she brought with her visions
of conquest. Buoyed by her words and
filled with a burning desire to honour the
gods, the warbands of many Marauder
tribes swore their allegiance to her,
allowing the Chainmaker to sail south,

to the shores of Westerland, in search

of glory.

But despite the victories she would tally
and the success she would enjoy, when
the Chainmaker first landed upon the
docks of Hollum, she did not lead an all-
conquering army. Instead, in the earliest
days of her invasion, Frydaal led the crews
of wolfships, alone or in pairs, to sack
settlements upon the remote northern
coast of Westerland. There, she put many
of Westerland’s inhabitants to the sword
and raised her accursed monuments from
the ashes of their homes.

Some of those unfortunate settlements
were protected by companies of militia,
armed men and women gathered together
to defend their homes from attack. Indeed,
the rare few were protected by garrisons
of State Troops but, most often, the towns
and villages Frydaal descended upon were
unguarded at best and utterly defenceless
at worst, and those battles she fought were
short, brutal affairs.

It was not until the Battle of Hollum, when
Elspeth Magritta, the Empress of Coin,
sent a large mercenary army northwards
into the Wasteland in a determined effort
to drive the Norscans from her realm that
Frydaal the Chainmaker fought her first
grand battle upon ground once claimed by
Sigmar for his Empire. There, at the Battle
of Hollum, the Chainmaker asserted her
strength and won a resounding victory,
driving the mercenary host before her into
the barren moors of Westerland.

The Razing Of Westerland

In the aftermath of the Battle of Hollum,
the army of Marauders the Chainmaker
had gathered pursued its routed enemy,
breaking up into warbands. Each such
warband was led by champions and
chieftains sworn to the Chainmaker'’s
cause, and eager to shed blood in honour
of the Ruinous Powers. This dividing

of her forces hastened the Norscan’s
advance inland and, whilst the number
of towns the Chainmaker held did not
greatly increase, many were the villages
throughout the Wasteland that fell to the
sword and great was the fear spread by the
wanton bloodshed.

When warriors of Grand Cathay arrived
upon the docks of Marienberg, sworn

to drive the invaders from the land and
led by none other than Miao Ying, the
Storm Dragon herself, they brought much
needed reinforcement to Westerland’s
armies. Upon disembarking her war junk,
the Storm Dragon's first command to her
officers was that they study maps and liaise
with locals, the better to identify a site at
which the Chainmaker could be brought
to battle.

Ultimately, Miao Ying chose to lead her
armies far from Marienberg, to Hollum,
now the seat of the Chainmaker’s power.
There, she would face her enemy in open
battle, retaking the town and driving the
Norscans back into the sea. But the march
to Hollum would not be easy.

In truth, drawing battlelines north of
Marienberg and awaiting the enemy
would have been far easier, but it would
have left many innocents vulnerable to the
Marauders’ advance. Thus did the Storm
Dragon command her warriors to cross the
Wasteland, crushing Norscan warbands
wherever they encountered them. Though
the work was bloody, it was a wise strategy
for, following the Cathayans' first victory
at Oostwald, even the smallest skirmish
would weaken the invaders and further
diminish their resolve.

campaign of battles great and small that was to persist for years to come. As bad as this was for the folk of Bretonnia, it
was equally calamitous for the people of Sigmar’s Empire, for the Dwarfs of the Grey Mountains, and even for the Elves in
their forests and on their islands, for Orcs are indiscriminate in their warmongering and generous with their aggression.

Waaagh! Spinecracker

‘Orc-Slayer’

In truth, this was not Bretonnia’s first encounter with Durgath
Spinecracker, for the towering brute had battled King Louen
before. The last time the Warlord had led his hordes against
Bretonnia’s chivalrous hosts, he had campaigned with all the
wit and cunning of any Orc Warboss; charging boldly across
the landscape without any particular destination in mind,
overrunning towns and besieging castles as they appeared
before him, seeking battle wherever he could find it.

Such brutal tactics and low cunning served the Warlord well
for a while. So unrelenting had Spinecracker'’s onslaught been
in those dark days that even the great walls of Couronne,

the unassailable seat of the Bretonnian kings, had proven

no obstacle, and the city had suffered great ruin in the
horde’s wake.

But whilst such ploys were assured to wreak havoc upon the
land and bring the Orcs into conflict with as many enemies

as possible, it had been a simple matter for the defenders of
Bretonnia to outwit and outmatch the Warlord. Whilst the
king had gathered his might to face Spinecracker in open
battle, lances of knights had encircled the horde in its headlong
charge towards distant glory, and numerous clashes had
divided the Orcs, spreading them thinly until, eventually, King
Louen brought Warlord Spinecracker to battle upon the plain
of LAnguille.

Whilst his knights slaughtered the diminished horde with
gleeful abandon, the king himself had duelled the Warlord
for many long hours, before eventually casting him down.
Defeated before his warriors, Spinecracker had fled and King
Louen had been hailed a hero, his quest to rid his lands of the
Orc menace fulfilled. In recognition of his feats, King Louen
was given the moniker ‘Orc-Slayer’ by his subjects, and work
began upon rebuilding Couronne after the destruction of war.

In the years since the culmination of the king's quest, whilst
peace had reigned and bold knights had spent their days
merrily hunting and jousting, the wily old Warlord had
nurtured his strength amongst the mountain peaks. In the
aftermath of his defeat, rivals had thought him weak, and
many an Orc had challenged Spinecracker. Yet none prevailed
and, with each passing victory, the Warlord claimed whole
mobs of boys, slowly rebuilding his mighty horde, though it
took him long decades to gather a host the likes of which he
had commanded before.

Thus, when drums pounded and Orc warbands poured from
the Pale Sisters, King Louen Orc-Slayer, fated to refight battles
he had already won, drew a weary sigh. For Spinecracker,
however, the campaigns ahead would be joyous. He had
learned much from past battles, and he was keen to show his
enemies who was boss.

A Gathering Waaagh!

Brutal, But Cunning

When Orcs and Goblins gather in large numbers to make war
against anyone and anything in their path, magical Waaagh!
energy builds around them, quickly disrupting the Winds of
Magic and creating a beacon of fury that can be sensed by
shamans many leagues away. In response, Orc and Goblin
tribes begin migrating towards the source of the disturbance,
instinctively drawn towards the promise of violence.

When Warlord Spinecracker led his second Waaagh! into
Bretonnia, not only were feral warbands unleashed to wreak
havoc upon the lands directly around the Pale Sisters, but

far distant realms were similarly thrown into turmoil by the
sudden increase in Ore and Goblin activity. Within mere
months of Spinecracker's first attacks on towns and villages in
the Marches of Couronne and the lands west of Marienberg,
countless warbands were roving through the Grey Mountains,
attacking isolated and fortified towns, and overrunning
Dwarf-held mines, some having travelled from as far afield as
the Border Princes and the Badlands.

One such warband was that of Drazgog Skullsmasha,

an especially egotistical Orc, accustomed to having the
smouldering embers of his burning ambition stoked by

the words of his loyal followers and Goblin Shamans. Boss
Drazgog felt the call to war and led his warriors northwards
from their stomping grounds in the Massif Orcal, towards
the Axe Bite Pass. Bruised from clashes with the Knights of
Quenelles and rival Orc warbands alike, Drazgog saw the
volume of heavily guarded mercantile caravans navigating the
pass between the many villages and hamlets that dotted its
length and saw great potential.

The infamous Axe Bite Pass spans the Grey Mountains
between the Empire and Bretonnia, a deep cleft through the
peaks guarded at its south by the great Bretonnian castle

of Montfort and at its north by the Empire-held fortress

of Helmgart. Many goods traded between the two nations
travelled through the pass, giving each much cause to keep the
region safe and secure. As a result, numerous small settlements
had sprung up along the banks of the River Schilder, which
flows from the peaks north of Montfort to Helmgart and
beyond. Populated by citizens of the Empire and Bretonnia
alike, these villages and hamlets serve the needs of the two
fortresses with their crops, livestock and other produce, and
prosper from trade with merchant caravans.

To the beady red eyes of Drazgog Skullsmasha, these caravans
and settlements looked ripe for the plundering, whilst the
armed warriors and knights that guarded the pass guaranteed
violence in abundance. The only problem the Orc foresaw was
Montfort itself, for whilst brutality came easily to Drazgog,
he did not lack for cunning and knew he could not hope to
take such a large citadel by force with the meagre warband at
his command.

With no idea that Helmgart even existed, the Orc led his
warband northwards through the mountains, reasoning that
travellers through the pass would be less well protected the
further they were from Montfort. Thus, late in the summer of
1C2276, Waaagh! Drazgog began to prey upon caravans and
settlements in the northern reaches of Axe Bite Pass, sowing
fear amongst the locals and setting the opportunistic brute on
a collision course with the defenders of Helmgart.

The Guardians Of Helmgart

Into The Mountains

When news of Waaagh! Spinecracker reached the defenders
of Helmgart, talk amongst the veteran soldiers turned to the
migratory nature of Orcs and Goblins. Upon learning of this,
many younger recruits theorised that Bretonnia’s woes may
benefit them, causing the number of warbands amongst the
peaks of the Grey Mountains to decrease. Their elders agreed,
but only to a point. No doubt those tribes that dwelled close
to the pass they guarded would uproot themselves, heeding
the call of war. But, they cautioned, the Knights of Bretonnia
needed to secure a swift victory. If they did not, ever more
tribes would be drawn from further afield towards the distant
war, and many of them would cross the Axe Bite Pass,

Worse, they warned, was that such migrations of Orcs

and Goblins lacked discipline and direction, and that the
wandering Warbosses, eagerly seeking out battles to fight,
would cast about for conquests of their own. Thus had the
Reikland, birthplace of Sigmar, been invaded more than once
before by the Orc and Goblin hordes that gathered in the Axe
Bite Pass in times of war.

Before summer's end, such concerns were proven to be

well placed. Outriders returned to Helmgart with word of

. merchant caravans burned, their guards slaughtered, and of
hamlets overrun, their populations scattered. Orc raiders
had indeed begun turning their gazes northwards, towards
the riches of the Empire, and only the stalwart defenders of
Helmgart stood in their way. As word spread and patrols into
the depths of the pass were increased, doom-laden devotees
of Sigmar began to gather at Helmgart's gates, convinced by
fanatical preachers that the End Times had come.

Axe Bite Pass

In response, the Castellan of Helmgart ordered increased
patrols, whilst turning a blind eye to reports of any officer who
allowed groups of Flagellants to accompany their warriors
into the perilous mountains. After all, should any rampaging
Orc horde pass beyond Helmgart and into the Reildand,
commoners by the score would be dragged into conflict.
Better, he no doubt theorised, to allow those touched by
Sigmar’s fury to vent their wrath in these early battles than

to turn them away from his gates and risk greater loss of

life elsewhere.

One such patrol fell under the command of Captain Gilbert
Weisgerber, a seasoned officer of the Reikland army and a
devout son of Sigmar who had served amongst the regiments
garrisoned at Helmgart for many years. Tasked with following
the river into the mountains and ensuring the safety of the
settlements that clung to its banks, Captain Weisgerber
marched with a band of swordsmen and handgunners, whilst
Outriders and scouts ranged ahead. As the soldiers began
their trek, a band of Flagellants hurried after them, and

the captain quietly thanked Sigmar for the company of his
righteous warriors.

Climbing towards the mountain peaks, the company passed
devastation: burned carts and hovels, butchered livestock,

and the sorrowful remains of civilians and warriors alike.

This was clear evidence of the wanton brutality of Orcs, but
hidden amongst the ruins were signs of even worse things, of
chaotic beasts prowling the forested slopes and preying upon
the weak. Such thoughts, however, were soon dismissed for,

as the company approached a narrow bridge spanning the
River Schilder, the unmistakable cacophony of Ore war chants
echoed from the slopes ahead.

The Duel On The Bridge

Forgotten Battles

At Weisnicht bridge, spanning a fast-flowing tributary of the
River Schilder, Captain Weisgerber’s company earned glory.
As the Orcs neared, Anselm Kemp, the sergeant of the State
Troopers, strode onto the rough wooden deck and raised his
sword in challenge, an act of courage inspired by legends of
Sigmar himself. Whilst the Ores hollered and jeered, Captain
Weisgerber surveyed their numbers, scratching careful notes
in his journal. When a hulking Orc burst from the mob
opposite, rushing towards the bridge with a savage cry, the
young sergeant held his ground, buying time for the runners
to escape behind him, carrying their captain's report back

to Helmgart.

For long minutes the combatants on the bridge duelled,

until Sergeant Kemp’s skill and speed proved greater than

his opponent's brute force, and the Orc was felled by a deft
riposte. In the clamour that followed, the waiting handgunners
and Outriders unleashed their firepower upon the indignant,
bellowing Orcs, their repeater handguns and long-barreled
rifles doing bloody work. Faced with this fresh challenge, fully
half of the Ore warband broke ranks and rushed forwards,
ignoring their Warboss’ commands to stand firm.

Whilst Kemp stood triumphant upon the narrow bridge, the
Ores rushed into the waters of the river. There, treacherous
footing and strong currents added to the toll taken by the
guns of the Reiklanders, and the impetuous brutes quickly
lost their appetite for battle. Turning tail and dragging their
wet carcasses back up the slippery banks, they fled past

their furious Warboss, disappearing into the trees. Thus did
the defenders of Helmgart win their first skirmish against
Warboss Drazgog, buying valuable time for reinforcements to
be summoned and slowing the Orcs’ advance.

In the months that followed, whilst Orcs from as far afield as
the Black Mountains and the Vaults encroached upon the Axe
Bite Pass in their northward migration, and whilst regiments
from the Reikland marched to swell the numbers garrisoning
Helmgart, Waaagh! Drazgog continued its rampage, clashing
often with the Knights of Montfort and the soldiers of the
Empire alike. With the numbers of armed Orcs and Men
swelling, it was only a matter of time before a tumultuous
battle erupted, driving the invaders back into the mountain
peaks, or sweeping the armies of the Reikland aside and
leaving the Empire vulnerable.

But it was not just Ores and Men that fought and died in
those calamitous months. Drazgog struck where he could,
his hunger for plunder and the thrum of Waaagh! energy
infusing the Grey Mountains driving him and his boys into
many battles. Dwarfs from Karak Ziflin were dragged into
the conflict, forced to defend the holdings of Man and Dwarf
alike. Even the Elves of Ulthuan, servants of the Sea Lord
Aislinn, who had travelled far inland via the Empire's great
rivers were forced to defend their caravans from Orc raiders.

Elsewhere, Captain Weisgerber faced Beastmen, disturbed
as the veteran soldier had feared by the migrating Ores and
driven to fury by the increased number of human soldiers
abroad in their mountainous territory. The final battle
between Waaagh! Drazgog and Captain Weisgerber’s company
would take place at the village of Gemelwald in the foothills
of the Grey Mountains. There, the dishevelled remnants of
Weisgerber’s company fought bravely to the last, defending
the villagers and hoping to prevent the Orcs from bursting
into the lowlands, where Drazgog’s violent campaign would
continue unabated into the heart of the Empire.

Forging A Narrative

Narrative Battles

The following scenarios tell the stories of small conflicts
within larger campaigns, desperate battles fought between
rival forces over crucial resources and locations. Such battles
are often left unrecorded in the histories and legends of the
Old World, but their outcomes can influence the fate of entire
armies. These scenarios are ideal for Battle March games (see
page 23), a fast-paced format played on battlefields measuring
no less than 30" x 44" and no more than 36" x 48', using

the core rules as laid out in the Warhammer: the Old World
rulebook, in which players field smaller armies, ranging from
400 to 750 points.

Players can choose one of the following scenarios to play,

or they can forge their own narrative using the deployment
maps, objectives and more contained elsewhere in this book. If
players cannot decide which scenario to play, simply roll a De
and consult the table below:

Linked Scenarios

Whilst each of the following scenarios can be played as a
stand-alone game, players can link their games together,
playing through the scenarios in the order they are presented
here, with each player maintaining their role as the attacker
or defender throughout. In this way, players can emulate the
events described previously, culminating in the defence of the
village of Gemelwald.

THE WEISNICHT BRIDGE

Mustering A Battle March

In games of Warhammer: the Old World — Battle March,
players field armies of between 400 points and 750 points.
Exactly what size an army should be depends upon what the
players prefer, and players are encouraged to play games with
different points values, rising in 50 point increments from 400
to 750, to find the size of the Battle March game they enjoy
the most.

Muster lists for Battle March armies are written as described
on page 276 of the Warhammer: the Old World rulebook.
However, there are a few key differences to bear in mind.

Note that, should players wish, these rules can also be used when
creating an Allied Contingent (see page 280 of the Warhammer:
the Old World rulebook) for a larger army.

Minimum Two Units

When writing your muster list for a game of Warhammer:
the Old World ~ Battle March, your army must include a
minimum of two non-character units (rather than the usual
three units). Unlike when writing a muster list for a larger
game, this minimum can include war machines but, as usual,
cannot include ‘swarms’ or ‘war beasts’

Character Limitations

As with any army, an army created for Battle March must
include at least one character to act as its General. However,
when writing your muster list, you may not spend more than:

+ 25% of your army’s total points value on a single character.

Unit Limitations

By their nature, the armies represented in games of Battle
March are unlikely to include huge regiments or large
numbers of elite troops. To represent this, when writing your
muster list, no unit may have a Unit Strength higher than 20,
and you may not spend more than:

+ 35% of your army’s total points value on a single Core unit.
+ 30% of your army’s total points value on a single
Special unit.
+ 25% of your army’s total points value on a single Rare or
Mercenary unit.

0-X Per 1,000 Points

Players of Warhammer: the Old World will already be aware
that many units or options are limited to ‘0-X per 1,000 points,
with ‘X’ being a number. This means that such options would
not normally be allowed when writing a muster list for an
army of less than 1,000 points. However, rather than preclude
such fun and characterful options entirely, when writing a
muster list for a game of Battle March, you may include a
single such option. This might be a unit, a character or even an
upgrade listed within a unit's options.

For example, a player writing an Orc and Goblin Tribes
muster list for a game of Battle March could include a single
Goblin Rock Lobber or Doom Diver, both of which are limited
to 0-1 per 1,000 points. Alternatively they could give a single
Goblin Mob the Skirmishers special rule, an option limited to
0-1 unit per 1,000 points.

SECONDARY OBJECTIVES

92) hilst the goal of the majority of conflicts is to see the enemy routed from the field and to secure locations of logistical
and tactical importance, there are often other angles being worked by agents on both sides. Be it the acquisition of a
particular resource, the elimination of an experienced enemy commander or striking out at the enemy's own supply chain,
there are many reasons for the vanguard of a campaigning army to meet their foe in battle.

Using Secondary Objectives

The secondary objectives that follow can be used in conjunction with any of the deployment maps and Battle March objectives
given previously. Should players wish, they can use one or both of the following secondary objectives. These are Raid & Burn and
Baggage Carts, and Victory Points are won for them as described on the following pages.

Raid & Burn

Baggage Carts

Oftentimes in war, victory can be found through clandestine
acts of subterfuge and sabotage, rather than through heroics
on the battlefield. Rations can be spoiled with poison and
waste, ammunition stores can be set ablaze and beasts of
burden can be slaughtered or set free — all of which can

have a lasting impact on an army’s ability to progress a
campaign. To represent this, Raid & Burn may be used as a
secondary objective.

Destroying Treasure Troves

A unit with a Unit Strength of 5 or more can attempt to
destroy a treasure trove by moving into base contact with

it during the Remaining Moves sub-phase. Once a unit is

in base contact with a treasure trove, it begins the process

of destroying it, scattering the riches to be found within or
spoiling food stuffs and ammunition. Whilst a unit is in the
process of destroying a treasure trove, it cannot shoot and can
only cast spells with a range of ‘Combat or ‘Self?

If, at the beginning of their next Start of Turn sub-phase,

the unit is still in base contact with the treasure trove, has a
Unit Strength of 5 or more, is not engaged in combat and is
not fleeing, the treasure trove is destroyed and removed from
the battlefield.

Victory Points: For each treasure trove a
unit destroys, its controlling player wins a
bonus of 30 Victory Points.

Caravans of food and equipment are the lifeblood of a military
campaign, and must be protected from attack as armies
traverse hostile terrain. To represent this, baggage carts may
be used as a secondary objective. If so, one or both players may
add a baggage cart to their army for the duration of the game:

+ Attack the Baggage: If only one player has a baggage cart, |
they are the defender and their opponent is the attacker,
tasked with destroying enemy supplies.

+ Guard Duty: If both players have a baggage cart, the
caravan guards of two enemy armies have crossed paths
with one another. Battle erupts, with the victor looting the
enemy supplies.

Victory Points: If a baggage cart is alive on the battlefield at
the end of the game, its controlling player wins a bonus of

25 Victory Points. Otherwise, their opponent wins a bonus of
25 Victory Points. In addition:

+ Ifa baggage cart is completely within the enemy's
deployment zone at the end of the game, its controlling
player wins a bonus of 25 Victory Points.

+ Ifa baggage cart manages to escape from the battlefield, its
controlling player wins a bonus of 25 Victory Points.

Tasked with the vital work of transporting much needed supplies, and able to offer little through

RANDOM HAPPENINGS

y Beat campaigns can lead warriors to encounter many strange places and situations. When roving warbands clash
with the vanguards of advancing armies upon unprepared ground, dangerous terrain, unexpected encounters with
ferocious monsters, unpredictable weather, and even the arrival of wandering mercenaries of questionable loyalties can all
conspire to complicate the battle. Such unexpected and disruptive occurrences are ideally suited to, and easily represented
in, games of Warhammer: the Old World - Battle March, making such games endlessly exciting and chaotic.

Oddities Of War

Using Random Happenings

The following pages present three tables, each of which serves
to highlight events that, for the most part, would have only the
most minor of impacts on a large-scale battle, or perhaps even
go unnoticed. But such events can have a dramatic influence
on the result of a smaller melee.

Disruptive Weather provides a game-long effect that can
greatly change the pace and flow of the battle, from swirling
‘wind storms that temporarily blind those they pass with
barrages of dust and dirt, to torrential downpours that

turn the ground underfoot into a quagmire of thick mud.
Wilderness Terrain helps to reinforce not only the danger
presented by the untamed wilds of the Old World, such as
patches of near-impassible briar and the lairs of lurking beasts,
but also unexpected blessings such as ready-made defences, or
wild plants with medicinal properties.

Finally, the Chaos of War table reflects the truly haphazard
nature of war. When used, stray baggage carts and wandering
mercenaries alike can suddenly appear on the battlefield,
turning the tide just as effectively as the dwindling of

ammunition or the unreliability of magic, and all in the blink
of an eye.

Before playing a game of Battle March, the players should
decide if they will include Random Happenings or not. If they
wish to include them, they should choose one of the tables on
the following pages: Disruptive Weather, Wilderness Terrain
and Chaos of War. If the players cannot agree which table to
use, simply roll a D3 and let fate decide.

Whilst it is recommended that players choose to use just one
of the Random Happenings tables for the sake of simplicity,
there is nothing to stop them using two, or even all three tables
at once, adding further variety and chaos. Each of the Random
Happenings tables is accompanied by guidance on when rolls
should be made and by which player.

Matched Play: If you are organising a Warhammer: the Old
World - Battle March matched play event (see page 42),

you may wish to include Random Happenings, possibly even
switching between tables each round. This is encouraged but,
for ease, which tables are in use should be decided beforehand.

Matched Play

BATTLE MARCH

Y

arhammer: the Old World - Battle March is a format very well suited to matched play events of the type discussed in
the Matched Play Guide. Games can be played in an hour or less, allowing for several rounds to be played in a single

day or evening, and both battlefields and armies alike are smaller than usual, giving players more space and making it

easier to travel to the venue.

Organising An Event

For the most part, a Battle March matched play event can be
run just like any other. Obviously, the Roles & Responsibilities,
as laid out in the Matched Play Guide, remain the same, as do
the Universal Rules and the details of Scoring & Tiebreakers.
There are, however, a few things for anyone organising a

Battle March matched play event to consider. The following
pages aim to address any uncertainties, whilst offering some
additional ideas to consider.

Army Composition

Whilst matched play events often include restrictions upon
muster lists, the general principles for Warhammer: the
Old World - Battle March already include a number of
such limitations (as described on page 23), making further
restrictions unnecessary.

Magic Items & Runes

Should you wish, the amount of points models can spend on
magic items or runes can be reduced at a matched play event.
For example, a 20% reduction would mean a model normally
able to spend 50 points on magic items would be limited to
just 40 points.

The Field Of Battle

Battlefields and terrain placement for games of Battle March
are described on page 24. However, whilst in most games it is

Scenarios & Secondary Objectives

Anyone organising a Battle March matched play event has
a choice to make when it comes to scenarios. Whilst the
standard Battle March objectives and deployment maps
presented previously are ideal, so too are the narrative
scenarios included in this supplement.

Organisers can also add secondary objectives to their
scenarios. As well as the secondary objectives found on
page 36, the following pages introduce a range of Secret
objectives, similar to those found in the Matched Play Guide,
but designed especially for Battle March.

Choosing Scenarios & Deployment Maps: As usual at a
matched play event, at the start of each round the organiser
must determine which scenario will be played and which
objectives and/or secondary objectives will be in use. Whilst
many organisers will simply choose deployment maps and
objectives, or scenarios, ahead of each round, choosing at
random from amongst those that have not yet been played at
the beginning of each round is also highly recommended.

Once a deployment map or scenario has been played, it cannot
be played again unless all of the other deployment maps or
scenarios have also already been played (in events that have
more than six rounds, for example), in which case, the process
begins again. By removing scenarios that have already been
played from the selection, players are assured to experience a
variety of different battles across the event.

Matched Play Muster Lists

As mentioned previously, Warhammer: the Old World -
Battle March is well suited to matched play, the smaller armies
required making travel easier and games quicker. Writing
muster lists for these smaller armies does, however, present
challenges, whilst also introducing new possibilities.

The challenges are, of course, that players must make tough
decisions when deciding which models to include in their
armies and which to leave out. This can result in players
wishing they had a few more points to spend in order to
include a favourite unit, but adding those additional points
and increasing the size of the games is not always the best
option. Battle March games are deliberately small and,
should the points limit increase too much, games become
longer and battlefields much larger, defeating the purpose of
the small format.

With this in mind, and should they wish, event organisers
can run matched play events where players are allowed

to bring not one, but two muster lists, enabling them to
field a broader variety of models from their collection.

On one hand, this allows players to write two very different
muster lists, each designed to excel in areas the other does
not. However, at the event, they must decide which list

to use prior to each game and, because such decisions are
made without knowing which of the two muster lists their
opponent will use, they risk choosing poorly. This presents a
conundrum; should each muster list be a well-rounded force,
capable of dealing with any enemy, or should each be more
specialised, relying upon the player's luck to field the right
army at the right time?

Two Muster Lists

At the event organiser’s discretion, players may be allowed to
bring two muster lists to a Battle March matched play event.
In such cases, the event organiser must make it clear to all
attendees well in advance.

Factions: Each muster list should be drawn from the same
faction. In other words, each player must choose a single
faction and then write two muster lists. A player cannot, for
example, bring one Empire of Man army and one Warriots of
Chaos army.

Composition Lists: Each muster list can be written using the
same composition list, or two different composition lists.

For example, an Empire of Man player might write one muster
list using their faction’s Grand Army composition list, and the
other using the Knightly Order Army of Infamy

composition list.

List Selection

Each muster list must be used at least once over the course

of the event. Prior to each game, players should share their
muster lists with their opponents, giving them time to study
each and making it clear which is number one and which is
number two. Then, each player chooses which list to use in
the game ahead in secret, both players revealing their choice
simultaneously (perhaps by making a note on a piece of paper,
or by revealing a concealed dice).

However, in the final round of the event, players that have not
already used both muster lists must use their unused list.

hile the overall objective of a campaign might be to conquer a new region of territory, liberate the land from a cruel

invader or vanquish a terrible foe, there are many other considerations taken into account by those in command.
Alongside these lofty ambitions, there are personal agendas that generals and kings seek to accomplish, sending bands of
daring heroes or gangs of misfit troops to enact their wishes. Meanwhile, conniving viziers and scheming wizards dispatch
their own agents to further causes unknown to their royal patrons and noble leaders.

Using Secret Objectives

Anyone organising a Battle March matched play event

may choose to use Secret objectives, adding further tactical
challenges for players and awarding additional Victory
Points upon their completion. The twist is that not all Secret
objectives are achievable by every army or in every game,
because with each passing round the players must choose a
single objective from a diminishing pool:

+ When writing their muster list(s) for a matched play event
at which Secret objectives are in use, each player must
choose a number of Secret objectives from those detailed
opposite, equal to the number of games scheduled.

+ At the start of each game, each player must choose one of
their Secret objectives and make a note of it on a piece of
paper, which should then be placed face-down at the edge
of the battlefield, in sight of their opponent.

+ At the end of the game, when calculating Victory Points,
both players reveal their Secret objective. If either has
achieved theirs, they win a VP bonus as stated in that
objective’s description.

+ After each round, when submitting results to the
scorekeeper, each player must also record which Secret
objective they chose for that round, and whether they were
successful in achieving it.

Players are free to remind themselves of their objective at
any time, but must ensure it is returned to the edge of the
battlefield once they have done so. Once a Secret objective has
been selected, it cannot be selected again for the remainder of
the event.

Pick-up Games

Should you wish, Secret objectives can be used in stand alone
games played amongst friends at a local club, often called ‘pick-
up games. To do so, randomly generate a pool of three Secret
objectives and choose one of them for the game ahead. In the
unlikely event that all of the Secret objectives you have drawn
are impossible to score, show them to your opponent and then
randomly generate a new pool of objectives.

The Importance Of Secrecy

As their name would suggest, Secret objectives should be kept
secret from your opponent until the end of the game when
calculating VP. This not only adds further drama and suspense
to the game, but also ensures that your opponent has to keep
guessing as to what your Secret objective might be. If you

are able to, feigning an attempt at a different Secret objective
can mislead your opponent and bait them into committing
resources to stopping the wrong Secret objective.

To fulfil this objective, you must have a unit with a Unit

At the beginning of the game, secretly nominate a terrain

If you fulfil this objective, you win a bonus of 25 VP.

Secretly nominate a friendly unit at the beginning of
the game. To fulfil this objective, this unit must be alive
and not fleeing at the end of the game, and have a Unit
Strength of 5 or more.

If you fulfil this objective, you win a bonus of 50 VP.

Duellist’s Flourish
To fulfil this objective, you must slay either an enemy
character or an enemy unit champion in a challenge.

If you slew an enemy character, you win a bonus of
30 VP. If you slew an enemy unit champion, you win a
bonus of 20 VP. You may only fulfil this objective once.

Gather Resources

To fulfil this objective, you must have friendly units
with a Unit Strength of 5 or more wholly within two
different rocky terrain features and/or woods at the end
of the game.

If you fulfil this objective, you win a bonus of 30 VP.

Slay The Beast

To fulfil this objective, you must kill a unit in your
opponent's army whose troop type is ‘monster; ‘monstrous
infantry’ or ‘monstrous cavalry: Any such enemy units that
have fled off the battlefield, or that are fleeing at the end of
the battle, count towards fulfilling this objective.

If you fulfil this objective, you win a bonus of 50 VP.

Magical Mastery

To fulfil this objective, you must successfully cast two
spells (including Bound spells) without having them
dispelled by your opponent, or dispel two of your
opponent's spells and without suffering a miscast yourself
during the same turn.

If you fulfil this objective, you win a bonus of 50 VP.

Hold Them Back!

To fulfil this objective, there must be no enemy units with
a Unit Strength of 5 or more inside your deployment
zone at the end of the game.

If you fulfil this objective, you win a bonus of 75 VP.

Seize The Tower

To fulfil this objective, you must have a friendly unit
controlling a strategic location or a building at the end of
the game.

If you fulfil this objective, you win a bonus of 40 VP.
